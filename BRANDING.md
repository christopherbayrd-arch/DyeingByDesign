# Dyeing By Design — Brand Kit & Growth Playbook

Your name, your slogan, your domain shortlist, and every idea worth stealing for
taking this from "shirts I make" to a real brand. Written August 2026.

---

## 1. The name

**Dyeing By Design** — your pick, and it earns its keep three ways:

1. **The pun works on sight.** "Dyeing" (correct craft spelling) tells people
   instantly this is hand dyed apparel, while the ear hears "dying by design,"
   which gives it just enough edge to be memorable.
2. **"By design" is the whole pitch.** These shirts look like accidents of
   nature, but every leaf is placed on purpose. Intentional. Deliberate. Designed.
3. **It scales past shirts.** Hoodies, totes, bandanas, workshops — nothing in
   the name boxes you into tees.

**Wordmark tip:** set it in a serif (the site uses Fraunces), and when you make
stickers or hang tags, stack it three lines tall: DYEING / BY / DESIGN.

## 5. Positioning — the two facts that sell these shirts

**Fact one: every shirt is physically impossible to duplicate.** Not
"limited edition" marketing talk — the spray literally cannot land the same way
twice. Lean into it everywhere:

- **Number every shirt.** A small hang tag: "No. 047 — the only one." Costs
  pennies, raises perceived value enormously, and gives customers something to
  post.
- Photograph each finished shirt before it ships. You build a gallery of proof
  and customers get "their" photo.

**Fact two: it's made from the Maine woods by hand.** Tourists, transplants,
and homesick Mainers all buy Maine. "Hand bleached in Maine with real leaves"
beats any graphic tee on a boardwalk rack.

## 6. The sleeper hit: custom "story leaf" shirts

The /custom page is set up for this. People will mail you leaves from:

- the tree they got married under (anniversary gifts)
- the yard they grew up in (sibling gift sets — one order, four shirts)
- a loved one's favorite tree (memorial pieces — handle gently, price fairly)
- their annual family hike or camp

This is the highest-margin, highest-meaning work you'll do, and none of it
competes on price. **Suggested pricing: $55 to $85 per custom piece** depending
on complexity, quoted per project (the site already tells customers to expect a
quote). When one goes well, ask permission to share the story — each one is a
marketing post that writes itself.

## 7. The hybrid model, put to work

Core designs (sumac, maple, oak, fern) stay made-to-order year round. Then a
few times a year, run a **numbered drop**:

- **The Dark Harvest drop** — October. Deep fall leaves, maybe maroon or
  forest-green blanks instead of black.
- **The Fiddlehead drop** — May. Fern season in Maine; tie it to fiddlehead
  festivals.
- **The Peak Week drop** — early October, foliage tourists at max.

Playbook per drop: announce to the email list first (the site collects
signups into your Neon database already) → post the making-of on social →
number each shirt → mark the drop sold out publicly. Scarcity you don't have
to fake, because you really did only make 20.

## 9. Pricing sanity check

- Gildan Heavy Cotton blanks run roughly $4 to $7; bleach is pennies. At
  $39.99 your materials are ~15% — healthy for handmade, and honestly you have
  room to go to $44.99 later once there's proof (reviews, numbered shirts).
- $5 flat shipping roughly covers USPS Ground Advantage for a single tee in a
  poly mailer; a second shirt ships nearly free in the same mailer — which is
  why bundles work: **2 shirts for $75, shipping included** feels generous and
  still nets more per order.
- Industry convention is +$2 for 2XL. Skipping it is fine at launch; just know
  it's normal if blank costs bite.
- Craft fair pricing: keep the same $39.99 in person. Never undercut your own
  website.

## 10. Marketing that fits this product

1. **Film everything.** The bleach reveal — spraying, then lifting the leaf —
   is exactly the kind of oddly-satisfying process video that travels on TikTok
   and Reels. 15 to 30 seconds, no talking needed, trending audio, post 2 to 3
   a week. This is the single highest-leverage free thing you can do.
2. **Fall family photo angle.** Search interest in "fall family photo outfits"
   spikes every September. Matching leaf shirts for the whole crew — post the
   idea in August/September.
3. **Local first.** Brunswick and Midcoast farmers markets, craft fairs, and
   holiday markets. A QR code on the booth banner pointing at the site turns
   browsers into online customers later.
4. **Consignment and wholesale.** Maine boutiques, state park gift shops,
   nature centers, and land trust shops are a perfect fit. Standard consignment
   split is 60/40; wholesale is typically 50% of retail, which is why a price
   bump to $44.99 eventually matters.
5. **Etsy as a side door.** Same products, Etsy's search traffic, and link
   your own site on the packing slip. Keep your site as home base.
6. **The email list is the asset.** Social reach is rented; the drop list is
   yours. Push signups at markets ("want to hear when the fall drop lands?").

## 11. Small ops touches that read "professional"

- **Care card in every package:** wash cold inside out, hang dry. Already on
  the site; print it business-card size.
- **Neutralize properly:** rinse, then a peroxide or bisulfite neutralizing
  bath before wash — stops the bleach from slowly eating fibers. Customers
  never see it; longevity is your reputation.
- Kraft tissue + poly mailer, numbered hang tag, handwritten first name on a
  thank-you card. Cheap, memorable, photogenic.
- Reply to custom requests within 48 hours; the admin page shows them the
  moment they land.
